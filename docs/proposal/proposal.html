1. Executive summary

Relevant records about multinational groups are distributed across RSL systems and other sources, including income tax returns, VAT returns, the withholding tax schedule, customs, PAYE, e-invoicing, refund letters and the company registry. An officer who wants to understand one company has to assemble that picture by hand, and a recurring VAT credit or a related-party charge looks very different once the other records are next to it.

Evidence-Led Case Review joins those records per company and turns each pattern into an evidence-linked case: what was observed, which records show it, which lawful explanations may apply, what the officer must establish, and a range for the exposure. It does not decide anything. Officers conclude; the system never does. A pattern is a reason to look, never proof, and a refund position alone never establishes abuse.

The same system serves the three users named in the challenge: risk analysts (where should I look?), refund auditors (can I process this refund confidently?) and Special Sectors auditors (what is happening in this company?). It keeps legitimate refunds moving through a "lower risk" lane and keeps the evidence, the decision and the reasoning together for the next review.

This proposal describes the full system we would deliver over a 12-month engagement, and separately shows what a working prototype already demonstrates. Every capability is tagged BUILT in the prototype, PILOT (to be delivered for the officer pilot), YEAR 1 or LATER, so RSL can see exactly what is proven today and what is committed. The prototype shows that we can deliver what we propose; it is not the limit of the proposal.

2. The challenge, and what we do about each part

RSL describes base erosion through related-party services and financing, in-house loss creation under the perpetual loss carry-over provision, VAT credits linked to unpaid VAT on imported services, expatriate employment income, and the line between independent consultancy and employment. The brief estimates about M1 billion in tax forgone each year; it calls this a preliminary workshop estimate, not a verified amount, and we make no claim about how much of it the system could recover.

3. The system we propose

3.1 Detection: 14 explainable rules, owned by RSL

CodePatternCodePattern
P01Persistent losses with group chargesP08Long-term personnel arrangement
P02Who funds the lossesP09Unreported fringe benefits
P03Fees growing faster than revenueP10Payments to low-tax jurisdictions
P04Related-party payments without matching WHTP11Large interest on group loans
P05Treaty rate claimedP12Profit squeeze
P06Imported services without matching VATP13Information gap (not a risk finding)
P07Recurring VAT credits (lower-risk lane)P14Possible data capture error

Each rule states what it examines, which records it needs, its threshold, why it matters, the lawful explanations, and the documents an officer may request. Thresholds are versioned settings, not code: RSL's rule owner changes them with a recorded reason, and a rule version is never edited after use PILOT: rule-owner editing screen. Missing records produce an information gap (P13), never a suspicion. A figure that looks like a keying error goes to data review (P14), never into company memory.

3.2 Evidence controls, enforced in code BUILT

  • Evidence ladder. A return alone: "may need examination". Return plus other RSL records: "appear inconsistent with each other". Plus outside records: "the group structure suggests". Plus taxpayer documents: "does not appear to match the evidence provided". The conclusion belongs only to the officer.
  • Every finding shows the observation, the lawful explanations, what the officer must establish, an estimated exposure range (never an assessed amount) and links to the exact source rows.
  • Sequencing. A taxpayer document stays invisible until an officer sends an information request naming it; it arrives after the stated response time.
  • Conclusions are stored as five parts: observed pattern, conclusion, evidence, conditions, reassessment point.

3.3 The officer's workspace

Each user group has its own desk and worklist (BUILT): analysts receive new filings, refund auditors see VAT credits and the lower-risk lane, Special Sectors auditors receive referred cases. A case carries its findings, evidence, a checklist, requests, documents, notes and a timeline; cases are ranked by estimated exposure and routed by rule. The officer records one of four outcomes, each with a reason: Concern supported, referred (not a finding of wrongdoing), Explained, legitimate, More information needed, or System error. Closing a finding as legitimate at or above M1,000,000 estimated exposure (configurable) needs a second officer's sign-off. Planned additions: officers adding a finding the rules missed and editing brief notes in place PILOT.

3.4 Company memory: informs, never exempts BUILT

Earlier decisions and their conditions are kept with the company. A later finding is still raised, with the earlier decision shown as context, and the system states when a pattern has moved outside the conditions of a decision. In the prototype, a fee increase at Qiloane is shown against decision DEC-0007, with the statement that the new fees fall outside its conditions.

3.5 AI assistance, grounded and optional BUILT

  • Case brief. The application builds a fact sheet of records it has looked up; the model writes a brief from that sheet only, each statement citing record IDs. A guard rejects any statement that cites an unknown ID, cites nothing, or breaks the wording policy, and a plain template brief takes over so the case page never fails. The officer approves, edits or rejects it.
  • Ask the records. An officer asks a question ("which companies had related-party fees rise more than 30%?"); the system finds the records first, the model may only word an answer from them, and every answer cites its sources. The guard also rejects any figure not in the facts, and a lookup answer is the fallback. It is a way into the same records, never a separate source.
  • Already working with a local model. The prototype is integrated with a locally hosted model (gpt-oss 20B) running on my own Mac mini M4 through Ollama. The web application reaches it over a private tunnel, and the case briefs and Ask the records described above are generated by it today, on fictional data.
  • Data stays inside RSL. The same local-processing design is intended for RSL's own infrastructure, so no taxpayer record leaves RSL. An approved cloud model is an option only if RSL's policy allows it; the provider is a setting.
  • Stronger numeric cross-checking of brief statements, and automatic redrafting when findings change PILOT. Semantic search across taxpayer documents YEAR 1.

3.6 Audit and notifications BUILT

Every receipt, analysis, case opening, request and conclusion is written to a tamper-evident audit log: entries are hash-chained, the database refuses edits and deletions, and a command verifies the chain. No blockchain is needed. Hand-offs send a WhatsApp message that carries no taxpayer details, only a case reference and priority, pointing the officer into the system.

3.7 Beyond the pilot

YEAR 1 A secure taxpayer response portal; scheduled feeds from eTax, eCustoms and Lekuka in place of file uploads, once RSL agrees; management reports on findings, outcomes and refund turnaround. LATER A data warehouse for history across tax types and years, and forecasting or exposure modelling once enough reliable outcomes exist. Potential exposure, assessed liability and collected revenue stay separate measures; none of this is claimed for the prototype.

5. Architecture, data and design for Lesotho

Figure 3. Records flow in once, stay unedited, and every claim downstream cites them. The model sees only the compact fact sheet for one case, never the database.

Data required

Confirmed with RSL in discovery; pilot uses approved extracts or masked history, not live access.

  • CIT and VAT returns; PAYE; WHT payment schedules
  • Customs imports; Lekuka monthly summaries
  • Refund claim letters; registration and group register
  • Past officer decisions, to seed company memory

Dependencies: RSL IT (eTax, eCustoms, Lekuka owners), the companies registry for group data, Meta for WhatsApp approval, and RSL hosting policy.

Designed for Lesotho

  • Limited connectivity: light server-rendered pages, no heavy front end, resumable uploads PILOT
  • Language: officer screens in English; Sesotho versions of notifications and taxpayer information-request letters PILOT
  • Record keeping: intake reads the formats RSL already uses (CSV, the emailed WHT Excel schedule, scanned letters)
  • Explainable: every statement cites a record; every outcome carries its reason

One person leads the workTwo juniors work alongside throughout; documentation and a handover pack; code and configuration available to RSL.
Honest about results. The prototype's data was written to contain known stories. It shows the workflow and the rules behave as designed: the 17 expected findings are produced with the expected records, the two ordinary companies and three lawful decoys raise nothing (apart from one lower-risk VAT result), and a full automated test suite passes. In a trial on 10 open cases with a local model, 9 briefs passed the guard first time and the guard caught the tenth. This does not show detection accuracy on real taxpayers. Only the pilot can.

Ownership. The call states that ideas generated at the event remain RSL's intellectual property. We accept that RSL owns its rules, thresholds, data, decisions and outputs; the licence terms for the platform software would be agreed with RSL before the pilot.
